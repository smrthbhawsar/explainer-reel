# Hooks and retention — shared by hinglish-explainer-reel and hinglish-3d-reel

Viewers decide in the first 1–2 seconds whether to keep watching, and then again every
10–15 seconds. The script has to win all of those moments, not just the first.

## 1. The opening line (0–3 s)

The **first sentence is the hook**. Rules:
- **≤ 12 words, under ~3 seconds spoken.** No greeting, no "aaj hum baat karenge", no
  "kya aap jaante ho" warm-up, no channel intro. Start mid-thought.
- It must contain **one concrete, surprising specific**: a number, a named thing, an
  unexpected object, a contradiction. "10GB data… ek fish tank se" beats "hackers are dangerous".
- It must **open a loop**: the viewer can't resolve it without watching. Don't explain in line 1.
- Hinglish, spoken naturally; English tech/brand words fine. End on a pause (`.` / `?` / `—`).

### Hook formulas (pick the one the topic supports best)

| Formula | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Shocking fact | "<Specific thing> ne <unexpected result> kar diya." | "Hackers ne ek casino se 10GB data chura liya — ek fish tank ke through." |
| Counter-intuitive claim | "<Common belief> galat hai." / "<X> actually <opposite>." | "Tumhara phone kabhi bhi sach mein 'delete' nahi karta." |
| What if | "Socho agar <impossible-sounding benefit>…" | "Socho agar PS5 games bina PS5 ke chal jaayein?" |
| Hidden mechanism | "<Familiar thing> ke andar ek <secret> chhupa hai." | "Har UPI payment ke peeche ek chhota sa race chal raha hota hai." |
| Stakes / loss | "Ek galti, aur <big concrete loss>." | "Ek galat click, aur poora bank account khaali." |
| Direct challenge | "<Thing viewer uses daily> — tumhe lagta hai tum jaante ho kaise kaam karta hai?" | "ChatGPT se kal ka sales data poocho — dekho kya hota hai." |
| Number tease | "<Number> <unit> mein <surprising outcome>." | "0.3 second mein tumhara paisa 4 banks se guzarta hai." |

Avoid: questions with an obvious answer ("Kya AI important hai?"), vague hype ("Ye video
tumhara dimaag hila dega"), clickbait the video doesn't pay off, fake numbers. **Every
claim in the hook must be true and paid off later in the script.**

## 2. The second line (3–8 s) — raise the stakes, don't answer

Follow the hook with one line that deepens the curiosity or makes it personal, then the
"Kyun?" / "Kaise?" beat. Only then start explaining.
> "Na computer hack hua, na phone. Ek fish tank. Kaise?"

## 3. Re-hooks every 10–15 seconds

Retention drops at every point where the viewer feels they "got it". Before each one, plant
a short **re-hook** line (2–3 in a 45–60 s reel, 3–5 in a 61–90 s one):
- Twist: "Lekin asli game yahan se shuru hota hai."
- Escalation: "Aur ye toh sirf pehla step tha."
- Contradiction: "Par yahan ek problem hai."
- Personal: "Aur ye tumhare phone mein bhi ho raha hai."
- Countdown: "Teesra step sabse crazy hai."

Keep them ≤ 8 words; vary them — never the same re-hook twice in one script.

## 4. Payoff and ending

- **Close the opening loop explicitly** in the last 15 s (callback to the hook's specific:
  the fish tank, the 10GB, the PS5).
- End on a **punchline** that reframes the idea in ≤ 10 words — a line people repeat or
  comment on. Optional soft prompt (one short line max): "Comment karo agar…" only if natural.
- Loop-friendly ending: if the last line flows back into the first, viewers rewatch.

## 5. Visual hook (applies to the composition)

- The **first frame already shows the most intriguing visual** of the reel (the fish tank,
  the glowing chip, the impossible object) — no fade from black, no slow establishing shot,
  no logo. Motion starts at frame 0.
- The hook's key word is a caption **keyword** (highlighted) and gets a visual beat on it
  (pop, zoom, label) anchored with `F("<word>")`.
- Something new happens on screen at least every ~2–3 s during the first 10 s.

## 6. What to deliver with every script

1. **3 hook options** (different formulas), each with the formula name, and your recommended
   one marked. The full script uses the recommended hook; the user can swap before recording.
2. The script, with re-hook lines in place.
3. A one-line note on how the ending calls back to the hook.
